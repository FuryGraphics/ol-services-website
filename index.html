Why it matters

A little upkeep goes a long way

Chimneys, stoves and vents do their jobs quietly. Most problems build up slowly, out of sight, and they're simple to catch with regular care.

Creosote buildup

Burning wood leaves creosote, a tar-like residue, on the inside of the flue. It builds up with use and is flammable, so a thick layer can ignite. Cleaning removes it before it becomes a problem.

Chimney fire risk

Cracked flue liners, damaged mortar and blockages can let heat reach combustible parts of the house. An inspection finds these issues while they're still straightforward to fix.

Carbon monoxide

Fuel-burning appliances make carbon monoxide, an odorless gas. A blocked chimney or vent can push it back into the home. Keep the venting clear and install working CO alarms.

Dryer vent lint

Lint that collects in the vent line restricts airflow and can catch fire. The U.S. Fire Administration lists failure to clean as a leading factor in home dryer fires.

The National Fire Protection Association (NFPA 211) recommends inspecting chimneys, fireplaces and vents once a year, and cleaning them as needed.

FAQ

Common questions

How often should my chimney be inspected or cleaned?

NFPA 211 recommends an inspection once a year. How often it needs cleaning depends on how much you use it and what you burn. The inspection will tell you whether it's due.

Do gas fireplaces need service too?

Yes. Gas doesn't leave creosote, but vents can still get blocked by nests or debris, and burners and pilot assemblies benefit from periodic cleaning and checks.

What are signs my dryer vent needs cleaning?

Watch for clothes taking longer to dry, a dryer that feels very hot on the outside, a burning smell, or lint collecting around the exterior vent hood.
