## Gas fireplace service

Gas fireplaces don't produce creosote, but they aren't maintenance-free. Vents can be blocked by nests or debris, and burners and pilot assemblies collect dust and lint over time.

A gas fireplace service includes:

- Cleaning the burner, pilot assembly and log set
- Checking and repositioning gas logs so the flame burns as designed
- Cleaning the glass front (on sealed units) and checking the gasket
- Inspecting the vent or chimney for blockages and damage
- Checking that the pilot and ignition work properly
- A reminder to test your carbon monoxide alarms

Smell gas? Leave the house and call your gas utility or 911 from outside first.

## Wood fireplace service

Traditional wood-burning fireplaces need the most regular attention, because every fire leaves creosote behind. Service covers:

- [Chimney sweeping](/services/chimney-cleaning/) and creosote removal
- Firebox inspection, including cracked firebrick and deteriorated mortar joints
- Damper operation and seal
- Smoke chamber condition
- Glass doors and screens checked

## Wood burning stove service

Wood stoves and inserts burn hot and efficiently, but their stovepipes and flues still collect creosote, and gaskets wear out. We:

- Clean the stove interior, baffle and flue collar
- Sweep the stovepipe and chimney or liner
- Check door and glass gaskets for a tight seal
- Inspect the stovepipe connections and wall or ceiling clearances
- Check the firebricks and baffle for damage

## Pellet stove service

Pellet stoves burn cleanly, but they produce fine ash that builds up quickly in the burn pot, heat exchanger and exhaust path. Neglected ash is a common cause of pellet stoves that won't ignite, burn poorly or shut down. Service includes:

- Clearing ash from the burn pot, firebox and ash traps
- Cleaning the heat exchanger tubes
- Cleaning the exhaust blower and venting
- Checking door gaskets
- Following your stove manufacturer's maintenance guidance

## A note on what you burn

For wood fireplaces and stoves, **burn only dry, seasoned wood**. The EPA's Burn Wise program recommends wood with a moisture content of 20% or less. Wet wood smolders, produces more smoke and leaves more creosote in your chimney.

Also keep in mind the **Sacramento Metropolitan Air Quality Management District's "Check Before You Burn"** program, which restricts wood burning on certain days during the winter season. Check their website for the day's status.
