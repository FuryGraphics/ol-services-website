## The three levels of inspection

The fire code standard **NFPA 211** defines three levels of chimney inspection. The right level depends on your situation, and we'll explain which one you need before we start.

### Level 1: routine annual inspection

The standard yearly check for a chimney that's in regular use, when nothing about the system has changed. We examine the readily accessible parts of the chimney exterior, interior, appliance and connection, looking for:

- Basic soundness of the chimney structure and flue
- Obstructions, nests and combustible deposits such as creosote
- Proper clearance from combustibles where it's visible

### Level 2: more detailed inspection

A Level 2 inspection includes everything in Level 1, plus accessible areas such as the attic, crawlspace and roof, and a **video scan of the flue interior**. NFPA 211 calls for a Level 2 inspection when:

- A home is being **sold or transferred**
- The system has **changed**. For example, a new stove or insert, a switch in fuel type, or a relined flue
- There's been an **event that may have caused damage**, such as a chimney fire, an earthquake, a storm or a malfunction

### Level 3: concealed areas

When a Level 1 or 2 inspection points to a hidden hazard that can't be evaluated otherwise, a Level 3 inspection may call for removing parts of the building or chimney (for example, a section of wall or the chimney crown) to reach concealed areas. It's rarely needed, and we'll always explain why before recommending it.

## What we check

- **Chimney cap and spark arrestor:** present, secure, not rusted through or clogged
- **Crown:** cracks or gaps that let water into the masonry
- **Brick and mortar:** spalling bricks, missing or crumbling joints, leaning or separation
- **Flashing:** where the chimney meets the roof, a common source of leaks
- **Flue liner:** cracked, shifted or missing tiles, and gaps at the joints
- **Smoke chamber and damper:** buildup, rust, and a proper seal
- **Firebox:** cracked firebrick or damaged mortar
- **Creosote and obstructions:** how much buildup there is, and whether cleaning is needed
- **Gas and stove venting:** connections, pipe condition and clearances

## What you get

A plain-language summary of what we found, photos or video where they help, and a clear explanation of which issues need attention now and which can wait. If repairs are needed, you'll get a quote with no pressure to book.
